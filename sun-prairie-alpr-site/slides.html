Automated License Plate Readers in Sun Prairie

Program overview, policy, cost, Wisconsin comparison, and how to participate

Prepared by Zack Mark — independent; not affiliated with the City of Sun Prairie or the Sun Prairie Police Department.
Last updated October 16, 2026 (newer: see the Updates tab)

Executive Summary

  • SPPD has used Flock plate-reader cameras since Dec. 2023 — 9 cameras (6 SPPD-owned, 3 donated by Woodmans).
  • The department credits them with stolen-vehicle recoveries and help in missing-person, shooting, and homicide cases.
  • The city is not renewing Flock and plans to move to Axon Outpost in 2027.
  • Chief Warych briefed the Public Safety Committee on Oct. 7 — a staff report, not a vote; no contract approved yet.
  • A non-binding Axon estimate puts the cost at ~$235,389 over 10 years (2027–2037) for 9 cameras.
  • Open questions: cost vs. benefit, audits, data sharing, contract control, scope creep.

What Is ALPR?

  • Squad-mounted readers since ~2009; fixed Flock network since Dec. 4, 2023.
  • 9 cameras: 6 SPPD-owned (end Dec. 31, 2026) + 3 donated by Woodmans (end Mar. 31, 2027).
  • Reads plates and vehicles only — no facial recognition.

How It's Been Used

  • Stolen vehicles: dozens recovered; some tied to weapons or drug seizures.
  • Missing persons / homicide: aided a 2025 homicide case and a 2026 shooting investigation.
  • Retail theft & fraud: including an armed robbery and a $79K ATM fraud case.
  • Hit-and-runs, fleeing vehicles, warrants: identified vehicles from partial or wrong plates.
  • Mutual aid: Madison, Deforest, Middleton, Verona and more.

From SPPD's own summaries (log updated through Sept. 21, 2026; 25 new items since May 22); not independently verified. The log lists only notable outcomes, so false alerts and misses aren't counted. It does include wrong plates, stale "stolen" flags, civil disputes, and a vehicle ruled out in a fatal hit-and-run.

Policy & National Standards

  • Policy F-18: background checks, alert verification, case-tied searches, 30-day retention.
  • No surveillance without reasonable suspicion; no monitoring by race, religion, gender, or politics.
  • Banned uses: immigration and traffic enforcement, harassment, personal use.
  • Sept. 23, 2026 Senate hearing: 13 national ALPR principles — SPPD says it follows all 13.

Oversight & Data Sharing

  • Periodic audits (roughly monthly to weekly) by a System Administrator.
  • Shared with 200+ agencies within ~50 miles.
  • No federal sharing without a court order or the Chief's approval.
  • Non-law-enforcement release needs a court order, subpoena, or Chief approval.

Cost & Contract Terms

  • 2023 order form: $15,000/yr for 5 units + $750 setup = $15,750 Year 1; $30,750 over 2 years.
  • Notes put current cost near $3,000 per camera per year (~$18,000/yr for six).
  • Flock owns the hardware and has final say on camera placement.
  • Flock itself may disclose footage to law enforcement or government when legally required.
  • Liability capped at 12 months' fees; excludes "repeat criminal activity."
  • Axon Outpost (non-binding estimate, Sept. 22, 2026): 9 cameras, 10-year term (May 2027–Apr. 2037), $235,389 total (~$23,539/yr), includes install, warranty, and two mid-contract hardware refreshes.
  • That quote itemizes ALPR/"Vehicle Intelligence" hardware and software only — no separate Fusus or livestreaming line item, though that's not confirmation those features are excluded.

Fine Print in the 2023 Flock Contract

  • Signed Oct. 6, 2023 by then-Chief Michael Steffes; 24-month term starts at installation, then auto-renews unless 30 days' notice.
  • Flock may change fees on 60 days' notice, use the city's name in marketing, and take ownership of any feedback.
  • "Morality" clause: Flock can terminate if the customer faces an indictment or scandal that tarnishes Flock's reputation.
  • Either side may assign the deal to a buyer or successor without consent.
  • Insurance Flock must carry: $1M/$2M general liability, $10M umbrella, $5M professional, $5M cyber.
  • Contract language mentions audio detection; the order form lists only the plate-reader camera.
  • Open question: the order form covers 5 cameras and a term from Dec. 2023, but reported end dates are Dec. 31, 2026 and Mar. 31, 2027.

Open Questions

  • Were alternative vendors considered? How is success measured?
  • Could scope expand later without public approval?
  • What happens if misuse is found?
  • Is the cost proportionate to the benefit? (A non-binding Axon estimate: ~$235K over 10 years vs. Flock's ~$15–18K/yr.)
  • Who has searched Sun Prairie's data, and why?
  • Chief Warych's Oct. 7 memo cites case law finding ALPR generally isn't a Fourth Amendment "search" — but a related case is still pending on appeal.

The Move to Axon

  • Chief Warych gave an informational report (no vote) to the Public Safety Committee on Oct. 7.
  • City says it would own and control its data; platform would include body cameras and TASERs.
  • Retention: Flock 30 days now; Axon discussed at 15–21 days.
  • Axon (NASDAQ: AXON): Scottsdale, AZ; founded 1993 as a Taser maker; now body cameras, evidence software, ALPR.

The Department's FAQ (Oct. 16, 2026)

  • City owns the data; Axon can't sell it. If the contract ends, the city has 90 days to retrieve it.
  • Each search needs an offense category and a reason; the log can't be deleted by users or admins.
  • Sharing only with approved state and local agencies under a signed MOU. No federal access; no agreements with 287(g) agencies.
  • Axon retention not set yet; a shorter period is being evaluated.
  • Not addressed: Fusus, livestreaming, old Flock data, AI training use, audit frequency.

Department statements of intent, not contract terms.

National Context

  • Feb. 10, 2026: an account in President Trump's name bought $1–5M of Axon stock.
  • Feb. 24, 2026: ICE sought ~17,800 Tasers (up from ~4,300), $220M over 5 yrs; specs reportedly matched only Axon.
  • No wrongdoing proven; the White House denies any conflict. Ethics experts cite the appearance of one.
  • A separate federal contract — not Sun Prairie's — but fair context for vendor questions.

Axon Outpost: Not Just a Plate Reader

  • Axon markets Outpost as 3-in-1: ALPR + livestreaming + vehicle-attribute recognition (make, model, color, roof racks, stickers).
  • Feeds into Axon Fusus, a platform Axon says can unify Outpost with other connected cameras/sensors.
  • Key question: which capabilities is Sun Prairie actually buying and turning on — not just "which vendor."
  • Does Policy F-18 (written for plate reads) automatically cover livestreaming and attribute recognition?

Flock & Axon: A Complicated History

  • 2020: Axon invested in Flock; Flock's tech was integrated into Axon Fleet 3.
  • Aug. 2024: companies agreed to a modestly expanded Fusus/Flock integration.
  • 2025: Axon unilaterally ended the API partnership; per Flock, no new mutual customers supported after July 24, 2025.
  • Worth asking directly: what happens to Sun Prairie's historical Flock data when the cameras come down?
