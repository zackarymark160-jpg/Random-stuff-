1.1 POLICY The Sun Prairie Police Department (SNPD) utilizes Automated License Plate Readers (ALPR) to enhance public safety, aid in criminal investigations, and improve law enforcement efficiency. ALPR capture infrared images of vehicle license plates, convert them to digital text using Optical Character Recognition (OCR) technology, and match them against databases of license plates of interest. OCR (Optical Character Recognition): Technology that identifies printed characters using photoelectric devices and computer software.

Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) Technology: Frequently Asked Questions

The Sun Prairie Police Department (SNPD [sic]) is committed to using technology responsibly to enhance public safety while respecting the privacy and rights of our community members.

As the department transitions from Flock Safety to Axon automated license plate reader (ALPR) technology, we recognize that residents may have questions about how the system works, what information is collected, who can access it, and how that information is protected.

The following frequently asked questions explain the technology, its intended use, and the safeguards in place to promote transparency, accountability, and responsible data management.

# Understanding the Technology

What is an Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR)?

An Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) is a camera-based technology that captures images of vehicles and their license plates as they pass a specific location.

ALPR technology provides law enforcement with an additional investigative tool to help identify vehicles associated with criminal activity, locate missing persons, and support other legitimate public safety investigations.

The cameras capture information at a specific point in time. They do not provide continuous, real-time tracking of vehicles or individuals.

What information do ALPR cameras collect?

The Axon ALPR system captures:

  • An image of the vehicle's license plate.
  • A broader image of the vehicle and its surroundings.
  • Basic visible vehicle characteristics, such as color, make, and vehicle type.
  • The date, time, and location where the vehicle was photographed.

Because the cameras capture images of the surrounding area, photographs may incidentally include nearby people, signs, buildings, or other objects.

However, the system does not use facial recognition technology or analyze images to identify pedestrians or vehicle occupants.

Can the cameras identify drivers, passengers, or pedestrians?

No. The ALPR system is designed to identify license plates and capture basic vehicle information— not to identify individuals. Although a person may incidentally appear in a photograph, the system does not perform facial recognition or use those images to identify drivers, passengers, or pedestrians.

The system also does not automatically retrieve registered vehicle owner information or create profiles of vehicle owners.

Can ALPR cameras be used to monitor speeding or issue traffic citations?

No. Neither the department's current Flock cameras nor the planned Axon ALPR cameras measure vehicle speed.

The Axon ALPR system does not currently have speed-detection capabilities, and there are no current or near-term plans to introduce them.

Why does the Sun Prairie Police Department use ALPR technology?

ALPR technology is one of several investigative tools used by the Sun Prairie Police Department to help protect the community.

It can assist officers with:

  • Identifying vehicles associated with criminal investigations.
  • Locating stolen vehicles.
  • Supporting investigations involving missing or endangered persons.
  • Developing investigative leads.
  • Identifying vehicles connected to serious incidents.
  • Helping investigators use available resources more efficiently.

ALPR technology does not replace traditional police work or independent investigative judgment. It provides additional information that may help officers investigate incidents and respond to public safety concerns.

Has ALPR technology made a difference in Sun Prairie?

The Sun Prairie Police Department has experienced meaningful investigative benefits from its use of ALPR technology.

The system has assisted officers in identifying vehicles of interest, developing investigative leads, and supporting criminal investigations.

The department has shared examples of successful ALPR-assisted investigations with the Public Safety Committee and Common Council.

While individual investigative successes do not independently establish that ALPR technology reduces overall crime rates, they demonstrate how the technology can support local law enforcement efforts. Why is the department transitioning to Axon?

The transition to Axon provides an opportunity for the department to continue using ALPR technology while strengthening its approach to data management, information sharing, and accountability.

Key considerations include:

  • Greater control over which agencies can access Sun Prairie's information.
  • Formal data-sharing agreements.
  • Restrictions on federal agency access.
  • Detailed audit records of system activity.
  • Continued City ownership of ALPR data.
  • An opportunity to establish a shorter data retention period.

The department's goal is to maintain a useful investigative tool while implementing safeguards that reflect the community's expectations regarding privacy, transparency, and responsible use.

# Data Ownership, Storage & Retention

Who owns the information collected by ALPR cameras?

The City of Sun Prairie owns and controls the information collected by its ALPR cameras.

This includes license plate images, vehicle photographs, associated data, and information generated through the system.

Axon does not acquire ownership rights to the City's ALPR data.

How long is ALPR information stored?

The Sun Prairie Police Department currently retains ALPR information for 30 days.

The final retention period for the Axon system has not yet been established. As part of the transition to Axon, the department is evaluating a shorter retention period that balances investigative needs with community privacy considerations.

Once the established retention period expires, the system automatically deletes the information in accordance with its retention settings.

What happens to the data if the City ends its contract with Axon?

If the City terminates its agreement with Axon, the Police Department will no longer be able to use the technology or access its data through the system.

The City retains ownership of its information and has 90 days following termination to retrieve its data. After that period, the data is deleted. The City can also export audit and activity reports for its records. Who pays to store the information?

The City pays a fee for each ALPR camera, which includes the cost of data storage.

The system automatically deletes information after the established retention period. This prevents the indefinite accumulation of data and helps maintain predictable storage requirements.

The City's data is not sold to third parties to offset storage costs.

Can Axon sell ALPR information to insurance companies or other businesses?

No. Axon cannot sell the City's ALPR data to insurance companies, private businesses, or other third parties.

The City owns and controls the information collected by its cameras.

The information is not collected for commercial purposes and is not available for sale.

# Data Access, Security & Accountability

Who can access Sun Prairie's ALPR information?

Access is restricted to authorized Sun Prairie Police Department employees, including sworn personnel and dispatchers, whose responsibilities require use of the system.

Access may also be granted to specifically approved state or local law enforcement agencies through a formal agreement with the City.

Each user receives access based on their assigned role and responsibilities.

Can Axon employees access the City's ALPR information?

Axon employees do not have unrestricted or ongoing access to the City's ALPR data.

Access may be temporarily authorized when the Police Department requests technical assistance. Any such access must be:

  • Specifically approved by the department.
  • Limited to the purpose of the support request.
  • Restricted to the time necessary to complete that request.

• Logged and available for audit. How is the information protected from unauthorized access?

The ALPR system incorporates multiple security safeguards, including:

  • Role-Based Access: Users can access only the features and information authorized for their assigned responsibilities.
  • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Users must complete an additional identity verification step when accessing the system.
  • CJIS Compliance: The system meets applicable Criminal Justice Information Services (CJIS) security requirements used to protect sensitive law enforcement information.
  • Audit Logs: System activity is recorded so the department can review how the technology is being used.

These safeguards help protect information from unauthorized access and support accountability.

Who is authorized to search the ALPR database?

Authorized Police Department employees, including sworn officers and dispatchers, may conduct searches for legitimate law enforcement purposes.

Access to the system does not mean an employee has unlimited authority to search the database.

All searches must comply with department policies and established restrictions.

What is required to conduct an ALPR search?

Every ALPR search must be connected to an authorized law enforcement purpose.

Before conducting a search, users must enter:

  1. An authorized offense category.
  2. A specific reason for the search.

The department determines which offense categories are permitted.

If a user selects an offense category that has not been authorized by the department, the system will not return ALPR data.

The offense category and stated reason are recorded in the system's audit history.

Are all searches logged?

Yes. Every search is logged.

Audit records allow the department to review system activity, including who conducted a search and the reason provided. This includes searches performed by users with administrative privileges. Can employees or administrators delete their search history?

No. Users cannot delete or modify their search history.

System administrators can review audit records but cannot alter or erase searches from the audit log.

This safeguard helps ensure accountability and supports the investigation of potential misuse.

What prevents an employee from using the system for personal reasons?

The department has established safeguards to help prevent and detect unauthorized use.

Every search requires a stated law enforcement purpose and is associated with an identifiable user.

Because searches are logged and cannot be deleted by users or administrators, the department can review activity and investigate potential misuse.

Using ALPR information for personal reasons—such as monitoring a former partner, acquaintance, or other individual without a legitimate law enforcement purpose—would be an unauthorized use of the system.

# Information Sharing with Other Agencies

Does the Sun Prairie Police Department share ALPR information with other law enforcement agencies?

Yes, but sharing will be limited, controlled, and subject to formal agreements.

Under the planned Axon system, the department intends to establish a more restrictive approach to information sharing than was available through its previous Flock system.

Information will be shared only with approved state and local law enforcement agencies that have entered into a formal Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with the City.

There will be no blanket sharing of ALPR information.

Will federal agencies have access to Sun Prairie's ALPR database?

No. The department will not share ALPR database access with federal agencies.

Axon's ALPR sharing system does not provide built-in or automatic federal agency access.

The Sun Prairie Police Department will also prohibit ALPR data-sharing agreements with agencies participating in the federal 287(g) immigration enforcement program. This restriction is intended to ensure that access remains consistent with the City's established public safety priorities and data-sharing requirements.

Can another agency share Sun Prairie's ALPR information with additional agencies?

No. The Axon system is designed to prevent agencies from redistributing ALPR data that they do not own.

The system uses a method known as federated sharing, which allows an approved agency to access authorized information without receiving ownership of a separate copy of the database.

An agency can share only ALPR data that it owns.

Can another agency use Sun Prairie's ALPR information to investigate crimes outside Wisconsin?

Potentially, yes, provided the search meets the requirements established in the formal data-sharing agreement.

Each participating agency must agree to the authorized uses of the information.

The City will not permit its ALPR data to be used for purposes that violate the agreement or fall outside the approved law enforcement objectives.

If an agency violates the terms of its agreement, the City will discontinue sharing ALPR information with that agency.

What happens if another agency requests ALPR information for an unauthorized purpose?

The department will not authorize database access for purposes outside its approved policies and data-sharing agreements.

Formal agreements will identify the expectations and limitations governing access.

Requests made through legal processes will be reviewed by the City and its legal counsel, as appropriate.

If Axon receives a legal request for the City's data, the company directs the request to the City as the data owner and discloses information only when legally required.

# Privacy & Community Concerns

Are ALPR cameras used to continuously track people?

No. ALPR cameras do not provide a continuous live feed of an individual's location or follow vehicles as they travel. Instead, each camera captures a vehicle and license plate when the vehicle passes that specific location.

During an authorized investigation, law enforcement personnel may search previously collected records to determine when and where a particular license plate was photographed.

These searches are limited to information available within the department's established retention period.

Could ALPR cameras be used to monitor visits to medical facilities or other sensitive locations?

The department recognizes concerns about the potential misuse of ALPR technology, particularly regarding visits to medical facilities, reproductive health providers, or other sensitive locations.

The ALPR system does not identify why a person visits a particular location or monitor an individual's activities.

However, a license plate search may reveal that a vehicle was photographed at a particular location and time.

For this reason, access to ALPR information is restricted to authorized law enforcement purposes, and searches are subject to audit and oversight.

The department's data-sharing agreements will also establish restrictions intended to prevent other agencies from using Sun Prairie's information for unauthorized purposes.

What safeguards prevent ALPR information from being used for activities that are not crimes in Wisconsin?

The department will establish formal data-sharing agreements defining how its ALPR information may be used.

These agreements will identify authorized purposes and prohibit uses that fall outside those requirements.

For example, the department does not intend to permit another agency to use Sun Prairie's ALPR database to investigate conduct that is lawful in Wisconsin solely because it is prohibited in another jurisdiction.

Agencies that violate the terms of their agreements will lose access to Sun Prairie's ALPR information.

Does the system create profiles of residents or their travel habits?

The Axon ALPR system does not automatically create behavioral profiles of individuals or registered vehicle owners. It collects individual vehicle detections at specific locations and times.

Authorized law enforcement personnel may search historical detections associated with a particular license plate for an approved investigative purpose.

Those searches are logged and subject to department oversight.

# Our Commitment to the Community

The Sun Prairie Police Department recognizes that public trust is essential to effective law enforcement.

We are committed to using ALPR technology in a manner that supports public safety while protecting individual privacy and promoting accountability.

Our guiding principles are straightforward:

Public Safety: Use technology to support legitimate law enforcement investigations and protect the community.

Privacy: Limit the collection, retention, access, and sharing of information.

Accountability: Maintain auditable records of system activity and establish clear expectations for authorized use.

Transparency: Provide residents with understandable information about how the technology works and the safeguards governing its use.

We welcome community questions and remain committed to evaluating our practices as technology, legal requirements, and community expectations evolve.

09/23/26 Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Crime and Counterterrorism hearing

-13 principles released by national law enforcement and public safety organizations (International Association of Chiefs of Police, National Sheriff's Organization, National District Attorneys Association, National Fusion Center Association, and National High Intensity Drug Trafficking Areas, and more) -[highlighted yellow]Sun Prairie Police Department currently follows all 13 principles.[/highlighted]

• [highlighted]Written policies[/highlighted]. Every law enforcement agency using ALPR systems must adopt and enforce clear written policies governing authorized uses, access, documented queries, verification, retention, sharing, data security, auditing, and accountability.

• [highlighted]Permitted uses[/highlighted]. ALPR must be used by trained and authorized law enforcement personnel only for legitimate law enforcement and public safety purposes.

• [highlighted]Authorized access[/highlighted]. System access must be subject to strong controls, routine auditing, and accountability for misuse—and promptly removed when an individual's duties no longer require it.

• [highlighted]Auditing and accountability[/highlighted]. Agencies must maintain access logs, conduct routine audits, and investigate potential misuse. Policy violations shall result in meaningful discipline, up to and including termination, consistent with applicable due process requirements. Suspected criminal misuse must be referred to the appropriate authorities for investigation and potential prosecution under applicable law.

• [highlighted]Required training[/highlighted]. Personnel with access to ALPR information shall receive initial and ongoing training on permitted uses, verification requirements, privacy and security protections, and the consequences of misuse.

• [highlighted]Documented queries[/highlighted]. ALPR queries must be tied to an official case number or another documented law enforcement or public safety purpose, with query and access logs maintained for auditing and accountability.

• [highlighted]Verification of plate matches[/highlighted]. ALPR information must be appropriately verified before enforcement action is taken. An alert alone does not identify the driver, establish guilt, or replace other investigative work.

• [highlighted]Confidentiality[/highlighted]. ALPR data collected for law enforcement purposes must remain appropriately protected from public disclosure. Comparable protections must apply when a private company stores or manages the information on behalf of a government agency.

• [highlighted]Meaningful retention[/highlighted]. Retention policies must preserve information needed for legitimate investigations while protecting privacy and security. The significance of a vehicle may not become apparent until weeks or months after a crime, particularly when a victim's report is delayed or an investigation develops over time. Strong access controls, documented queries, and auditing are more effective safeguards

• [highlighted yellow]Responsible data sharing[/highlighted]. Law enforcement must be able to responsibly share and query ALPR information across jurisdictions because missing people, victims, dangerous offenders, and criminal activity do not stop at jurisdictional boundaries.
